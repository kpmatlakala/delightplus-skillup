Using the Moderation Guide

# 1\. Introduction

The purpose of this Moderation Guide is to provide the Moderator with basic tools to conduct moderation before, during and after assessment has taken place.

The Moderation Guide incorporates a system of comparative moderation as a basis for obtaining more objectivity in the moderation findings, and allows for block moderations instead of a “per portfolio” approach only.

## 2.2 Moderation Documents

### 2.2.1 Pre-Moderation

The pre-moderation should be conducted during and directly after learner registration. When conducting the pre-moderation, delete the concurrent / post moderation section and only complete the pre-moderation reports.

### 2.2.2 Concurrent / Post Moderation

The concurrent moderation should be conducted at the end of every Learning Unit and results reported as part of the SETA moderation report. When conducting the concurrent moderation, delete the pre-moderation section and only complete the pre-moderation reports.

# 3\. Characteristics of the Moderator

1.  The Moderator must be able to identify and solve problems, e.g. plan for contingencies, handle the moderation of assessments for candidates with special needs, handle problems that arise during moderation and suggest changes to moderation following review.
2.  The Moderator must be able to work as a team member, i.e. work effectively with Assessors and other relevant parties during moderation as well as post moderation
3.  The Moderator must be able to organise and manage oneself and ones activities when planning, preparing, conducting and recording the moderation.
4.  The Moderator must be able to collect, organise and critically evaluate information. This characteristic is important when gathering, evaluating and judging evidence as well as the assessment process.
5.  Good communication skills are essential when the Moderator communicates with Assessors and other relevant parties during moderation, and when providing feedback.
6.  The ability to understand the impact of moderating assessments on individuals, organisations and the credibility of recognition through the NQF systems is essential.
7.  The Moderator must be able to plan, conduct and give feedback on moderation in a culturally sensitive manner.
8.  The Moderator must be confident and able to keep discussion focused and guide conversation to keep dialogue flowing smoothly
9.  The Moderator must be non-judgmental and able to encourage expression of all viewpoints without showing preference or sharing personal views
10.  The Moderator must be well informed and understand and is familiar with the subject
11.  The Moderator must be comfortable with all types of people and different communication styles

## 3.1 Fundamental Background Knowledge

The Moderator needs to have a thorough understanding of the following:

1.  Principles of assessment.
2.  Principles and practices of RPL.
3.  Methods of assessment.
4.  The principles and mechanisms of the NQF.
5.  Assessment policies and ETQA requirements.
6.  Moderation Techniques, systems and specific moderation requirements.
7.  The role and function of a Moderator.
8.  Knowledge of quality assurance and policy and procedure.
9.  Understanding of organisational or institutional contexts.

## 3.2 Functions of the Moderator (In a NUTSHELL):

1.  To verify that Assessments are fair, valid, reliable and practical.
2.  To evaluate assessment design, assessment process, assessment outcomes. Including documenting proof of individual learners’ competence status.
3.  To evaluate the performance of registered Assessors.
4.  To identify and provide evidence for the de-registration of Registered Constituent Assessors.
5.  To identify areas of improvements within the Assessment System.
6.  To identify the need to redesign assessments, assessment tools/instruments if required.
7.  To identify the need to redesign moderation, moderation systems, tools if required
8.  To provide an appeals procedure for dissatisfied Learners.
9.  To provide feedback to SETQAA on Unit Standards and Qualifications.
10.  To provide support and guidance to the Assessors.

## 3.4 Internal Moderation & roles of the internal moderator

Internal Moderation occurs at training provider level. The Services SETA accredited providers are required to have registered Constituent Moderators for fulfilling this requirement. All internal Moderation Processes shall be defined and documented within the Provider’s Assessment and Moderation Policies and Procedures.

These internal moderators should:

-   Establish systems to standardize assessment, including the plans for internal moderation
-   Monitor consistency of assessment records.
-   Through sampling, check the design of assessment materials for appropriateness before they are used, monitor assessment processes, check candidates evidence, check the results and decisions of assessor for consistency.
-   Co-ordinate assessor meetings.
-   Liaise with external moderators.
-   Provide appropriate and necessary support, advice and guidance to assessors

## 3.5 External Moderation

External Moderation is conducted at ETQA Level, on a random sample of 25%. It usually involves:

1.  Confirming provider accreditation status
2.  Structured curriculum
3.  Implementation mechanisms of the provider’s QMS
    1.  In particular – elected workplaces provider learners with the required exposure as per the qualification / learnership / skills programme requirement
4.  Learning material and relevance to the qualification / learnership / skills programme
5.  Constituent status of assessors and moderators
6.  The provider’s assessment guides indicating the following
    1.  Design of assessment activities and the process that will be followed
    2.  The provider’s Assessment Tools that will be used for formative assessments
    3.  The provider’s Assessment Tools that will be used for summative assessments
7.  Portfolio of Evidence (PoE) indicating the following review of formative assessment
    1.  The work being assessed is the learner’s own work - authenticity
    2.  summative assessments
    3.  Progress and Assessor reports
8.  Learner records database and upload of enrolment / achievements
9.  The provider’s moderation guides indicating the following
    1.  Design of moderation activities and the process that will be followed
    2.  Moderation plan
    3.  Sampling requirements
    4.  Moderation Report
10.  The internal moderation report
