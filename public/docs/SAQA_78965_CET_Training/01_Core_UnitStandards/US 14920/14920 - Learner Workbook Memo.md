## Activity 1 (5 marks) – Identify major issues that teams face

*Any 5 of the following:*

1. Lack of clear goals – team members don’t understand what they are supposed to achieve.  
2. Poor communication – information is not shared effectively, leading to misunderstandings.  
3. Conflict among members – personal disagreements or competing interests hinder progress.  
4. Unequal participation – some members dominate while others remain silent (social loafing).  
5. Lack of trust – members are reluctant to share ideas or admit mistakes.  
6. Poor leadership – no one coordinates tasks or resolves issues.  
7. Resource constraints – not enough time, budget, or tools to complete the task.  
8. Unclear roles and responsibilities – members don’t know who does what.

## Activity 2 (10 marks) – Describe and explain how one can contribute to team problem solving

*10 distinct contributions:*

1. **Listen actively** – Pay attention to others’ ideas without interrupting. Show respect.  
2. **Share relevant information** – Provide facts, data, or experiences that help the team understand the problem.  
3. **Ask clarifying questions** – Help the team explore the problem deeper by asking “what”, “why”, “how”.  
4. **Suggest possible solutions** – Brainstorm ideas without judging them too early.  
5. **Build on others’ ideas** – Say “yes, and…” to develop suggestions further.  
6. **Offer constructive feedback** – Politely point out potential flaws and propose improvements.  
7. **Keep focus on objectives** – Remind the team of the goal when discussions drift off topic.  
8. **Volunteer for tasks** – Take responsibility for specific actions (e.g., gathering data, testing an idea).  
9. **Summarise progress** – Help the team track what has been agreed and what remains to be done.  
10. **Support team decisions** – Once a solution is chosen, commit to implementing it even if it wasn’t your preference.

## Activity 3 (5 marks) – Describe the suitability of the Nominal Group Technique (NGT) in problem solving

Nominal Group Technique (NGT) is a structured method for group decision making that gives every member an equal voice. It is suitable for problem solving because:

1. **Prevents domination** – Members write down ideas silently before discussion, so louder or more dominant members cannot influence others immediately.  
2. **Generates many ideas** – Everyone contributes independently, increasing the quantity and variety of solutions.  
3. **Uses voting to prioritise** – Ideas are ranked or voted on by all members, leading to a democratic decision.  
4. **Reduces pressure** – Shy or less confident members feel safe to share because ideas are anonymous or written first.  
5. **Works well for controversial issues** – NGT reduces conflict because the process is structured and transparent.

> **Note:** NGT is especially suitable when the team has members of different status or personality, and when the problem requires creative or sensitive solutions.

## Activity 4 (5 marks) – Outline the benefits of working as a team

*Any 5 benefits:*

1. **Diverse perspectives** – Different backgrounds and experiences lead to more creative and robust solutions.  
2. **Shared workload** – Tasks are divided, reducing individual pressure and speeding up completion.  
3. **Better decision making** – Multiple people check each other’s reasoning, reducing errors.  
4. **Skill complementarity** – One member may be good at analysis, another at communication, another at technical tasks.  
5. **Increased motivation and accountability** – Team members encourage each other and feel responsible to the group, not just themselves.

## Activity 5 (10 marks) – Outline qualities that make one contribute effectively to a group / team functions

*10 qualities:*

1. **Reliability** – Completing tasks on time and as promised.  
2. **Respect for others** – Valuing different opinions and treating everyone courteously.  
3. **Active listening** – Understanding before responding.  
4. **Empathy** – Recognising and considering others’ feelings and constraints.  
5. **Adaptability** – Willing to change approach when new information arises.  
6. **Problem-solving attitude** – Focusing on solutions rather than blame.  
7. **Clear communication** – Expressing ideas simply and directly.  
8. **Accountability** – Admitting mistakes and learning from them.  
9. **Cooperation** – Putting team success above personal glory.  
10. **Initiative** – Acting without being told when something needs to be done.

## Activity 6 (10 marks) – Identify ways of building a collaborative team environment

*10 ways:*

1. **Set clear common goals** – Everyone must understand and agree on the team’s purpose.  
2. **Define roles and responsibilities** – Avoid confusion by clarifying who does what.  
3. **Establish ground rules** – For example: “no interruptions”, “one person speaks at a time”.  
4. **Encourage open communication** – Create safe spaces for sharing ideas and concerns.  
5. **Celebrate small wins** – Acknowledge progress to build momentum and morale.  
6. **Resolve conflicts quickly** – Address issues directly and fairly before they escalate.  
7. **Use team‑building activities** – Short exercises that build trust and familiarity.  
8. **Share information transparently** – Keep everyone updated on progress and changes.  
9. **Provide constructive feedback** – Focus on behaviours, not personalities.  
10. **Lead by example** – Team leaders should model collaboration (listening, sharing credit, helping).

---

## Activity 7 (8 marks) – Outline important roles that contribute to the working of a group or team

*8 roles (based on common team role models):*

1. **Leader/Coordinator** – Guides discussion, assigns tasks, keeps the team focused on objectives.  
2. **Recorder/Secretary** – Takes notes of decisions, action items, and deadlines.  
3. **Timekeeper** – Monitors the schedule and alerts the team when time is running out.  
4. **Researcher/Information gatherer** – Collects data, facts, or expert opinions needed for decisions.  
5. **Analyst/Critic** – Evaluates ideas for strengths and weaknesses (constructive devil’s advocate).  
6. **Implementer/Doer** – Carries out practical tasks such as writing, coding, building, or organising.  
7. **Supporter/Motivator** – Encourages participation, reduces tension, and celebrates efforts.  
8. **Liaison/Communicator** – Interfaces with outside stakeholders (other teams, management, clients) and reports back.
