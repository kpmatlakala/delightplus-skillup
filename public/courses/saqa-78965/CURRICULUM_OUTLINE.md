# SAQA 78965 — Curriculum Outline

> **Qualification**: Further Education and Training Certificate: Information Technology — Systems Development
> **SAQA ID**: 78965 · **NQF Level**: 4 · **Credits**: 165 · **Duration**: 1 year
>
> **Delivery note (2026 intake)**: The current CET intervention is delivered in a **block-based** structure (Block 1 → Block 2 → Block 3), which is the conventional approach for this qualification and aligns with the existing lesson plans (`Block-1`, `Block-2`, `Block-3` LessonPlan.md). **Future intakes may move away from a strict block model** while still recognising the block-based interventions already in flight. ## 1. Programme Overview

### Purpose
Build a foundational entry into Computer Sciences and Information Technology, specifically Systems Development, covering the basic knowledge needed for further study at Higher Education Levels.

The qualification can be acquired through formal study **or** in the workplace via learnerships — addressing the historical "no experience, no job" gap by allowing workplace experience to be gained while qualifying.

A qualifying learner is a **well-rounded entry-level Systems Developer** with fundamental IT knowledge plus interpersonal and business skills, prepared for later specialisation.

### Progression Pathway
- Entry-level employment in the computer industry (e.g. junior programmer, junior project team member).
- Foundation for further study — articulates into:
  - National Certificate: **Systems Support** (NQF 5)
  - National Certificate: **Systems Development** (NQF 5)
- Credits from National Certificates in IT (NQF 2 & 3) count as assumed learning.
- Flexible structure for use in Learnership Schemes across the IT sector and other sectors where IT is a key requirement.

### Exit Level Outcomes
A learner will be able to:
1. Communicate effectively with fellow IT staff and users of information systems.
2. Demonstrate understanding of different types of computer systems and the use of computer technology in business.
3. Demonstrate understanding of problem-solving techniques and apply them in a technical environment.
4. Demonstrate understanding of Computer Technology Principles.
5. Demonstrate understanding of Computer Programming Principles.
6. Work effectively as a team member within a development project environment.
7. Carry out, under supervision, a small-size task demonstrating fundamentals of Computer Programming.

### Critical Cross-Field Outcomes
Effective written/verbal communication; understanding of computer systems & business use; identifying and applying problem-solving techniques; computer architecture / networking / OS / data-storage understanding; producing program segments; team participation; building a program that combines fundamental programming outcomes.

### Entry Requirements
Competence at the FET band up to **NQF Level 3**, including:
- Numeracy NQF 3
- Literacy NQF 3
- Communication NQF 3
- Ability to use a PC competently
- Unit standard **14911** "Participate in formal meetings" (NQF 2)

Exposure to computing is an advantage, not a requirement.

### Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL)
Learners may be assessed without further formal study. RPL requires submission of a **portfolio of evidence** in a prescribed format. Assessor and learner jointly agree the assessment procedure (subject to ETQA rules). Assumed learning must be assessed before any qualification-level assessment.

## 3. Design Strategy

### Clarity of focus on outcomes (design-down)
Outcomes are broad, comprehensive, **assessable and observable** indicators of learner achievement at each stage. Programmes include both specific outcomes and Critical Cross-Field Outcomes (e.g. problem-solving, adaptive ability). All learning is **designed back from the outcomes**.

### Theoretical content
Content is connected to real-life situations and authentic tasks. Programmes blend theoretical, practical and (where possible) experiential learning. Delivery allows flexibility of methods, environments and resources.

### Teaching & learning
- **Learner-centred**: learners take responsibility for reaching the outcome; emphasis is on outcome attainment, not procedure.
- **Multiple opportunities** to demonstrate competence.
- **High participation & co-operative learning**: inductive activities, group/team/pair work, debate, role-play, research, experimentation. Constant feedback and affirmation through assessment.
- **Integration**: theory ↔ practical/workplace, critical thinking, reflection and action.

### Assessment principles (SAQA)
- Methods: **appropriate, fair, manageable, integrated** into work or learning.
- Evidence: **valid, current, authentic, sufficient**.
- Process: **systematic, open and consistent**.
- **Criterion-referenced**, derived directly from the unit standard.
- **Continuous & integrated**: formative (during learning, no credits) and summative (after learning, for credits).
- Variety: simulations, portfolios, self-assessment, workplace assessment.
- Discriminates between low and high levels of achievement; advancement is on demonstrated achievement.

### Evaluation
Three layers — **Facilitator** (clarity of outcomes, illustration of key points, adult-learning principles), **Programme** (achievement of outcomes, relevance, sequence/pace), **Logistics** (room, seating, refreshments, accommodation).

- **Aids**: flash cards, learner guides, handouts.

## 5. Learner Support Strategy

### Learner responsibilities
Active participation in activities and discussions; planning and preparing; asking for support; meaningful input and constructive challenge; finding better ways of working; complying with assessment requirements.

### Provider responsibilities (Facilitator / ECF / Assessor / Coach / Mentor)

**Before**: confirm purpose and prerequisites are understood.

**During**:
- Provide all materials and resources.
- Guide and support learners through skills practice.
- Ensure outcomes are met per unit-standard requirements.
- Identify struggling learners and intervene.
- Conduct **formative assessment** and take corrective action.

**After**:
- Workplace coach assists with structured on-the-job activities (Workplace Guide).
- Conduct workplace formative assessment.
- ECF helps the candidate prepare evidence for submission and judges readiness.
- Assessor plans the assessment, conducts it, gives developmental feedback, makes the assessment judgement.
- Assessor supports re-assessment and communicates the appeals policy.
